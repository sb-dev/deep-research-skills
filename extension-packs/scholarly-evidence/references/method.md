# Scholarly evidence method

Use the work or study as the synthesis unit and reports/versions as representations. Record which finding belongs to which representation; a second report is not a second independent study. Earlier reports remain eligible when they answer a historical or methodological question. A later report can change the population or configuration without invalidating every earlier finding.

Before acquisition, choose question-relative eligibility and appraisal. Extract actual design, task/population, comparator, metric, denominator and relevant uncertainty from the inspected representation. An abstract cannot supply unseen methods. Examine eligible adverse findings. Compare like configurations before combining findings, and distinguish a benchmark's operational threshold from an application's acceptance criterion. Propose application-specific verification when transfer is untested.

On refresh, identify affected report/claim dependencies, preserve unaffected findings and keep historical locators. For an audit, assess unit identity, current/report status and method transfer separately from citation syntax and core support.

Method basis: [Cochrane Handbook chapter 4, §4.2.3](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-04), inspected 2026-09-13, chapter updated March 2025. It distinguishes studies from their multiple reports and the need to link reports. Its primary setting is intervention reviews: that linkage principle transfers, but clinical appraisal instruments and exhaustive-review claims do not automatically apply to computing research. The remaining rules are this pack's question-relative application of core evidence and inference requirements, not a claim of clinical or systematic-review certification.
