# Handoff to UX Design

Revision r2, 2026-09-13. Input: [research.md](research.md) r2 and [programme-map.md](programme-map.md). These are research recommendations awaiting audit, not validated user needs or an approved interaction design. The UX owner owns research methods, recruitment, accessibility assessment and design choices.

| Research basis | Design question to test | Proposed observation of success, not a measured result |
|---|---|---|
| C1/C5 contextual finding aids | How can a reader follow creator/series context without confusing the holder with the creator? | Reader can explain which description level they are viewing and why a related record is relevant. |
| C2/C3 missingness | How should a no-match result explain the search boundary and possible unknowns? | Reader distinguishes “no match in this search” from “no surviving record” and from “event did not occur”. |
| C4/C6 representations | Can a reader tell whether they saw an original image, machine text, human transcription or research interpretation? | Reader can return from a claim to the inspected region and recognise a disputed reading. |
| C7/C8 access and reuse | How can available, restricted, redacted and permission-pending information remain understandable? | Reader identifies what they may inspect and which intended use still needs an owner decision. |
| C10 uncertainty/revision | What communicates competing interpretations and a corrected transcription without hiding previous reasoning? | Reader can identify what changed, which claim is affected and whether its review is current. |

Candidate tasks for an owner-led pilot: locate a record through its collection context; inspect a claim's representation; explain an unsuccessful search; compare two readings; notice a correction. Use a synthetic or cleared public fixture until the item/use permissions are settled. No recruitment, interview, contact, private data collection, usability session or accessibility test occurred in this research.

Unknowns: intended users and their archival experience, disability/access needs, preferred terminology, tolerable context detail, languages, emotional/cultural implications, and the practical burden of maintaining evidence records. Institutional help pages describe catalogue conventions; they do not establish that a proposed interface works for users. A research lead and archivist should review candidate tasks and wording before real-user work.

Carry original references and page/region locators alongside any prototype content. Do not portray prototype uncertainty values as calibrated probabilities or imply professional verification with a badge. Layout, navigation, visual encoding and copy decisions belong to UX. Reopen the handoff when the audience, archival scope, access state or interpretation changes. The review/decision owners are listed in [review-needs.md](review-needs.md).
