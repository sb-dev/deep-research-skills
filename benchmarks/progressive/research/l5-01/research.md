## Findings and recommendation

The domain warrants a bounded pilot around contextual discovery and accountable interpretation. Its central research unit should distinguish a record from its catalogue description and each accessed representation. Finding aids are routes into an archive, and their coverage and access limits belong with any finding. A search failure cannot establish that an event did not happen. These are research recommendations grounded in C1–C8 below, subject to archival review; they do not choose an architecture.

For this single programme, core research with explicit project instructions is enough to produce the present dossier. Across future archival investigations, a reusable contextual-search and representation-appraisal procedure is sufficiently distinct to justify the **archival-context 0.1.0-draft.1 proposal** in [pack-qualification.md](pack-qualification.md). Its value over a careful core prompt remains untested. Neither existing catalogue profile is an adequate archival profile merely by sharing provenance terminology.

The programme and handoffs are [programme-map.md](programme-map.md), [handoff-software.md](handoff-software.md), [handoff-ux.md](handoff-ux.md) and [review-needs.md](review-needs.md). ## Contradictions, alternatives and unknowns

X1: S5 How to Search says multiple keywords combine with AND; its Searching for Names example describes unquoted names with OR. Both statements were inspected in the same page. No live query was run to decide which describes the present catalogue. Do not bake either rule into software or evaluate research completeness on it. Proposed repair: software/research owner checks official current search semantics and one authorised bounded query pair before implementation.

X2: S2's flat born-digital piece presentation qualifies a simplistic assumption that every digital reference encodes a paper hierarchy. Preserve the original guidance's scope and date. NARA's description hierarchy and TNA's practice are institution-specific views, not contradictory universal rules. The programme needs a mapping study rather than an imposed common hierarchy.

X3: TNA general website reuse language is qualified by explicit image and third-party exceptions; NARA government-work guidance is qualified by mixed holdings and other rights. These are within-source scope qualifications, not evidence that everything is cleared or prohibited. C7 retains both.
