### Three connected workstreams

**W1 user/editorial problem and alternatives.** C01 and C05 establish a verification responsibility and a demonstrated attribution failure mode. They do not measure a small technical team's frequency, review time, dissatisfaction or budget. Proposed users are technical editors and analyst-authors responsible for explainable updates. Proposed beneficiaries are decision makers who need to revisit the support; these roles are hypotheses, not customer discovery. Compare a human-written brief with Zotero notes against supplier citation tools and a curated official-source digest. C02–C04 are strong adverse evidence: useful functionality is already available. A newsletter reader may prefer brevity over a full trace, and an employer may already fund an analyst; both remain untested competing explanations.

**W2 technical traceability and bounded refresh.** Inference C13 from C06–C09: a small record-based prototype is plausible, but full automated trust is not established. A source record should preserve original URL, observed representation and dates, access depth and rights; an evidence record should identify passage/page and context; a claim should record its exact qualified proposition, support/counterevidence and reviewer state. A dependency list lets one changed input reopen affected claims and briefs. Source bytes or ETags identify versions, not accuracy. Parsing, lost links, tables, duplicate syndication and unsupported inference remain failure risks. A human can author this structure with existing files; no engine repository was needed or treated as demand proof.

**W3 permitted use, review and publication.** C08/C10–C12 support separating read, retain, process, quote and redistribute permissions. Prototype with owner-authored or explicitly licensed sources; for ambiguous material retain a locator and minimal notes, not a redistributed full copy. The present package uses bounded paraphrases and links; it does not license a downstream corpus. A receiving editor must verify each material claim against accessible support, preserve uncertainty, sign off the exact output and choose disclosures/corrections. Customer terms, source terms, privacy and jurisdiction remain their owners' decisions. No current price has been included or inferred from old study prices; no legal clearance is claimed.

### Baseline decision and open gaps

C14 recommendation (inference, conditional): test whether review/refresh transparency saves enough real effort beyond incumbents to justify a service. Value assumption: traceability and safe correction matter sufficiently to users to outweigh reading and review overhead. G02 user need, G03 payer/procurement, G04 repeat willingness to pay, G05 actual engineering effort and G06 source-specific permitted use remain open.

Proposed smallest next work after consumer authorisation: compare two briefings on one narrow technical decision using identical permitted sources, with a manual/Zotero baseline; have an editor check support and record time, corrections and omitted counterevidence. Repeat on one real update. Then test whether an authorised payer chooses the service over continuing the baseline. Advance only with repeat use, fewer consequential errors or materially lower review time, feasible maintenance and a rights-compatible funding path. Stop or pivot to training/internal tooling if incumbents suffice, reviewers redo all work, citations create unwarranted confidence, refresh misses affected claims or rights/review costs dominate. No numerical demand forecast is warranted.
