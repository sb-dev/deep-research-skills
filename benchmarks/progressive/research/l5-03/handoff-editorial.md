# L5-03 handoff to creative/editorial production — R1 baseline, 2026-09-13

**Decision to retain:** a brief remains an authored judgement requiring verification of its exact text and permitted use. [research.md](research.md) owns all evidence and limitations. No legal clearance or external publication is authorised.

R1 policy basis is specifically AP's 15-August-2023 original (C01), which prohibited staff use of ChatGPT to create publishable content and treated output as unvetted. Do not use this historical rule as a current universal ban. G01 requires reading the newer AP original already identified in discovery before final policy synthesis.

For a later internal trial, use an owner-authored or explicitly licensed corpus. Require each material factual statement to have a locatable support passage; label supplier assertions, inference and unresolved conflicts. Keep a visible observed period and correction note. Test whether shortening the brief removes limitations or contrary evidence. An appealing citation is not support, and an authentic source can still be wrong.

Before publishing, the editorial owner chooses the audience, signs off the exact revision, confirms source-use permissions with the responsible rights owner, and sets a corrections route. Public reading, excerpts, images, cached copies, model processing and redistribution are different uses. AP, Google, W3C and third-party works have distinct policies; no blanket licence follows from this report.

Proposed acceptance experiment: inspect every material claim in one short sample against original support and record correction/review time, including a changed-source update. Return unacceptable claims to the evidence owner and clarity problems to the writer. You own narrative, design, disclosure, attribution and publication judgement; software tests and business enthusiasm cannot substitute for that decision.
