## Answer and scope

A useful fictional watershed needs stores, transfers, delays and imperfect observations. Water availability has separate quantity, timing, use and quality dimensions. A fictional temperate upland-to-lowland basin is the production premise, not a sampled real watershed. The four workstreams and their consumers are in `programme-map.md`; the authoritative claim-to-evidence relationships are in `mechanism-evidence-map.md`.

**Physical processes (C1–C2).** Represent precipitation, snow, soil moisture, groundwater and river storage with transfers between them. Snowmelt and groundwater discharge can connect storage to later surface flow. A stock is not a flow; a hydrograph's rapid response need not mean all discharge is newly fallen rain. The Swiss isotope study estimates an age-related fraction from measured isotope seasonality, rather than directly observing every water parcel. [USGS water-cycle description](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/water-cycle-png), [von Freyberg et al. 2018, §§2–3 and Fig.1](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/22/3841/2018/).

**Observation limits (C3–C4).** Preserve what is measured and what is inferred: a stage–discharge relation is a particular rating relationship; precipitation-frequency estimates are statistical results tied to a location, duration and historical data. A map grid does not create observations in every cell. The NOAA volume is a historical methodological source, not a 2026 climate-risk assessment. Its future stationarity premise must not be silently transferred to a changing fictional climate. [USGS measurement source](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/how-streamflow-measured), [NOAA Atlas 14 Vol.2 v3.0, §§3.1–3.3](https://www.weather.gov/media/owp/hdsc_documents/Atlas14_Volume2.pdf).

**Land use and human interactions (C5–C6).** Keep channel yield separate from other benefits. The review supports a conditional land-cover effect; it does not supply a universal percentage change for a forest tile. Human diversions, storage and pollution sources are plausible controls, but their local strengths remain uncalibrated. The bounded follow-up adds C6: infiltration and peak-response metrics can move differently from channel yield; this supports a conditional fictional tradeoff, not a prediction that one intervention produces all three outcomes together. [Filoso et al. 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0183210), [USGS diagram description](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/water-cycle-png).

**Scenario variation (C7).** A dry spell after snow storage, a storm on a wet catchment, upstream withdrawal and forest expansion are alternative fictional premises. Vary forcing, initial storage and management separately before combining them. These are proposed comparisons; no outcomes were generated. Production tests and real-world transfer requirements are in the three owner handoffs.

## Units and abstraction contract

Proposed bookkeeping, not an executed or empirically calibrated model: over a chosen interval, ΔS = P + I − ET − O − W, where all terms are volumes (m³), S is the total defined in-basin water store, I/O are all boundary inflows/outflows, and W is net consumptive withdrawal excluding returns already accounted for. Count each boundary transfer once. Infiltration and snowmelt internal to that basin merely move water between substores; they are not additional external inputs. A groundwater boundary need not coincide with a surface divide, so the fictional boundary must explicitly state its exchange assumption. This last boundary choice is a design assumption pending hydrogeological research.

For a future production model choose one area and clock: area km², stores m³, flux rates m³/s, rainfall depth mm per stated interval. Conversion identity: 1 mm over 1 km² = 1,000 m³. Multiplying a rate by the interval gives a volume; a monthly sum cannot represent an event peak. These are dimensional identities and proposed accounting choices, not evidence that the future model is realistic.
