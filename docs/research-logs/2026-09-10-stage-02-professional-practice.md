## Research method and provenance

This was a purposive comparative desk review of primary professional guidance, institutional standards and selected original methodological research. It was not an exhaustive systematic review, a practitioner survey or an experiment. Normative guidance establishes what an institution prescribes, not how frequently practitioners comply or whether its method improves outcomes. No interviews, studies, provider runs or benchmark measurements were performed by this bootstrap.

Discovery began with the evidence families named in bootstrap section 3, then expanded to cover gaps in the nine streams. Official source material was preferred; useful references were followed; fuller sources were sought where abstracts or generic ethics codes were insufficient. The [source register](2026-09-10-stage-02-sources.md) records 42 accessed items, their original URLs, dates where established, inspected scope and access limits. PDF passages were read with visual inspection where needed; no OCR or numerical result extracted from an unread figure is claimed.

### Independence and stopping assessment

Forty-two source entries do not mean forty-two independent confirmations. Cochrane chapters share an editorial framework; JBI pages form one manual; UK government guidance cross-refers within a common analytical system. PRISMA and SWiM are reporting resources, not independent empirical demonstrations of conduct quality. Hosted copies, revised pages and institutional corrections retain their originating relationships. The comparison therefore reasons about complementary responsibilities and disagreements, not votes among citations.

Research stopped after each of the nine traditions had identified primary evidence, every requested dimension had a bounded finding or explicitly labelled adaptation, and all seven questions could be answered without claiming unsupported specialist competence. Further searches were used to repair the initially thin competitive-intelligence evidence, not to inflate the source count. Remaining limits concern generalisability, access depth and method effectiveness; none is concealed as a completed empirical validation.

## Answers to the seven governing questions

These answers are analytical conclusions from the method matrix. They are not a single universal research protocol.

### Q1: Exhaustive search versus purposive coverage

Match the search claim to the method and intended inference. A declared systematic evidence review needs defensible comprehensive discovery and selection; a bounded fact check does not become better merely by accumulating unrelated sources. Qualitative conceptual work can justify purposive selection, while a scoping map still needs a transparent search boundary. The researcher must disclose exclusions and resource limits; budget exhaustion does not convert a selective search into an exhaustive one. See M1-M2 and M8, D03-D05. [S02, S06, S14.]

### Q2: Sufficiently direct and credible sources

Ask separately what the source directly establishes and how trustworthy that establishment is. A company's document can establish its stated policy without proving the policy's effect. A preserved image can establish what a file contains without proving its date or context. Credibility is claim-relative: origin, access, expertise, method, incentives, temporal fit and contrary material all matter. No title, publisher or primary-source label removes the need to examine the inference. See M1, M5-M7 and M9, D06-D07. [S01, S23, S33, S42; examples are analytical distinctions.]

### Q3: Assessing source independence

Follow the evidence towards its originating observation, study, dataset or witness. Different URLs, editorial teams or model outputs are not independent observations by themselves. Separate independence of acquisition, evidence and review: two reviewers can check one source without creating two sources. Record shared inputs and residual uncertainty; seek a new evidence path only where the material claim needs it. This follows the repeated-unit and information-quality distinctions, rather than imposing a fixed source quota. See M1 D08-D09, M6 D08-D09 and M7 D06-D09. [S07, S24, S26, S29.]

### Q4: Multiple reports of one study or event

Keep the report and underlying unit distinct. First remove literal duplicate records; then relate multiple publications, versions or accounts that concern the same unit. Do not discard a secondary report that contains useful supplementary details, and do not count it as another experiment. For events, link the shared event while retaining whether accounts came from genuinely different observers. Uncertain identity should remain unresolved rather than force a merge. See M1-M2 D08 and M4 D08. [S03, S07, S20; event linkage is the bootstrap's adaptation.]

### Q5: Negative, null and contradictory findings

Eligibility must not depend on whether a result supports the preferred answer. Preserve a reported null result, an opposing effect and an access gap as different states. Investigate whether disagreement is real or caused by different definitions, periods, settings or methods. Where it remains unresolved, separate the findings and explain its consequence for confidence and use. Selective visibility is itself an evidence-base limitation. A forced consensus or a majority of significant results is not a repair. See M2 D10-D12 and M8 D10-D16. [S12, S32.]

### Q6: Safe acceleration and specialist review

Candidate acceleration targets are bounded, checkable operations: locating records, duplicate-record suggestions, structured extraction drafts, reference checks and highlighting inconsistent values. Safety depends on retained originals, testable error detection and a competent reviewer for consequential interpretation; no acceleration benchmark was run here. Causal synthesis, contested authentication, sensitive collection, translation-dependent claims and high-stakes recommendations require appropriate expertise and authority. Institutional restrictions still apply: Bellingcat's policy rejects generated article copy and requires human verification of AI-assisted research. Its human editorial standard is not satisfied by this AI-written bootstrap. See M1 D13, M5 D13 and M8 D13. [S05, S09, S25, S30.]

### Q7: Decision-ready rather than merely comprehensive

A decision-ready research output makes its relevance inspectable: the question answered, the viable interpretations or options, the support and limits, the uncertainty that could change the choice, and the evidence still missing. It can be short when the evidence need is narrow. Comprehensiveness measures coverage; readiness additionally requires fitness for the specified use. The consumer retains value judgements and action authority. See M3 D03/D14, M7 D14 and M8 D02/D11/D14. [S16, S18, S22, S28; Stage 1 boundary.]

## Production implications without premature workflow design

The following are design conclusions from the comparison, not empirically measured cost savings. They address the family requirement to examine cheap representations, consequential commitments and repair scope.

| Stream | Cheapest useful working representation | Commitment or costly step needing justification | Operation character and review boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Reviews | Protocol, search pilot, eligibility examples and a piloted extraction form. | Broad retrieval, screening, appraisal and formal synthesis. | Search/record handling is partly mechanical; eligibility, bias and method decisions remain judgement-heavy. |
| M2 Synthesis | Evidence-grouping table and explicit candidate interpretation. | Statistical pooling or theory-building that changes the strength of the conclusion. | Calculation can be deterministic; compatibility and interpretation need method competence. |
| M3 Commercial intelligence | Decision-question map with known and unknown evidence needs. | Paid research, human-source contact or a consequential commercial conclusion. | Collection and routing are tool-dependent; value criteria and business commitments remain consumer-owned. |
| M4 Technology landscape | Tested query, unit definition and a small cleaned corpus or signal register. | Large corpus acquisition, entity normalisation and strategic extrapolation. | Counting is repeatable only after judgement about corpus and units; patents are not sales measurements. |
| M5 Investigation | Lead record, original-material locator and explicit verification questions. | Sensitive acquisition, fragile-source exposure or public allegation. | Preservation is partly deterministic; authentication, ethics and consequences require specialist judgement. |
| M6 Verification | Exact claim plus context and a source-check plan. | Publication of a verdict affecting people or organisations. | Reference checks can assist; editors retain responsibility for support, context and correction. |
| M7 Tradecraft | Assumption list and competing explanations. | A consequential forecast or judgement adopted by a customer. | Alternatives can be generated, but evidence discrimination and confidence are not resolved by fluency. |
| M8 Policy | Analytical brief, evidence map and proportionate assurance plan. | High-impact analysis, commissioned evaluation or adoption in policy. | Verification and fitness-for-use validation are distinct; the assurer cannot merely repeat the analyst's claim. |
| M9 Archives | Creator/collection map and precise record references. | Travel, paid copies, difficult transcription or contested historical inference. | Retrieval and transcription tools assist; missingness, authenticity and context require interpretation. |

Relevant professional evidence and transfer limits are in the corresponding matrix profiles; these are not prescribed universal artefacts or a mandatory sequence.

### Decisions and rejected alternatives

Adopt question-relative method choice, explicit units, accessible provenance, contrary evidence, qualified uncertainty and targeted repair as candidate reusable responsibilities. Preserve differences in search strategy, appraisal, inference, publication and specialist review. These refine Stage 1's quality boundary without changing who owns the downstream decision.

Reject a universal systematic-review pipeline: it would misrepresent bounded verification and purposive interpretative work. Reject unstructured browsing followed by prose: it cannot reliably expose selection, dependence or transformations. Reject a generic truth score: it would conceal the difference between methodological quality, contextual relevance, event likelihood and confidence. Reject mandatory multi-agent imitation of professional teams: separate model outputs do not establish independent expertise or evidence.

No provider, core workflow, numerical quality threshold, skill inventory or pack catalogue is selected. Stage 3 owns the question/evidence/claim model; stages 4-6 own source acquisition, workflow and effort mechanics; stages 7-13 own provider, skills, packs, examples and evaluation design. These are original stage allocations, not deferrals of unfinished Stage 2 requirements.
