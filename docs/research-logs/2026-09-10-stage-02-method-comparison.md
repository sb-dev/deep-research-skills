# Stage 2 Method Comparison Matrix

Authority: governing bootstrap section 8. All nine research traditions and all seventeen requested dimensions are addressed below. Sources and inspected locators are in the [source register](2026-09-10-stage-02-sources.md).

**E** means a documented prescription, description or finding in the identified source, not a claim that every practitioner follows it. **A** means this bootstrap's analytical adaptation, explicitly distinguished from the source. A source that does not prescribe a particular mechanism is not treated as evidence that its discipline has none. These profiles compare professional responsibilities; they do not select a core workflow, technology, skill inventory or fixed team size.

## Comparative overview

| Tradition | Appropriate question and characteristic unit | Search posture | Distinctive limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Systematic and scoping reviews | Systematic: bounded evidence question; scoping: distribution and character of evidence. | Protocol-led broad discovery with explicit eligibility. | Mapping is not an effect estimate or a recommendation; reporting completeness is not conduct quality. [S01, S06, S08, S10] |
| M2 Research synthesis | What a body of evidence supports, or how experiences and mechanisms are understood. | Comprehensive or purposive, justified by synthesis purpose. | Incompatible evidence cannot be made compatible by fluent narrative. [S12, S14, S15] |
| M3 Market and competitive intelligence | What a decision-maker needs to know about the relevant competitive environment. | Need-led collection, retaining room for unexpected signals. | Client priorities can cause blind spots; an insight is not authority to commit the business. [S16, S39, S40; Stage 1] |
| M4 Technology landscape | Where technological activity, alternatives and emerging signals lie. | Defined patent corpus plus appropriate non-patent and horizon sources. | Patent-family counts and weak signals do not directly measure commercial success. [S20, S21] |
| M5 Investigative and OSINT research | What can be established from traceable digital observations. | Iterative leads, verification and preservation. | Preserved bytes are not proof of authenticity or of the alleged event. [S23, S24] |
| M6 Journalistic verification | Whether a specific public factual claim is supported in context. | Claim-led checking and independent support. | Publication pressure does not justify a verdict when evidence is insufficient. [S26] |
| M7 Analytical tradecraft | Which explanation or future judgement is defensible under uncertainty. | Gap-driven evidence and explicit alternatives. | Consistent sources can share a faulty premise; likelihood and confidence differ. [S28, S29] |
| M8 Policy and decision support | Which evidence informs a specified public decision or evaluation need. | Proportionate methods tied to intended use and timing. | Assurance does not transfer value judgements or policy authority to the analyst. [S30, S31; Stage 1] |
| M9 Historical and archival research | What surviving records can establish about a past setting or interpretation. | Provenance-aware movement across collections and finding aids. | Catalogue absence, missing records and missing events are different propositions. [S33, S34, S36] |

## M1: Systematic and scoping reviews

| ID | Dimension | Professional finding and bounded implication |
|---|---|---|
| D01 | Roles | E: JBI identifies content, review-method and search expertise, independent reviewers and knowledge users; a librarian strengthens discovery. [S05] |

| D03 | Research protocols | E: An a priori scoping protocol identifies the intended method; subsequent changes need justification rather than retrospective concealment. [S04] |
| D04 | Source discovery | E: Sensitive, documented searches extend beyond convenient databases when the question requires grey literature; web stopping needs advance consideration. [S02, S06] |

| D06 | Source appraisal | E: JBI does not routinely require risk-of-bias appraisal for scoping unless the question needs it; effect-oriented certainty requires more. [S08, S13] |
| D07 | Evidence extraction | E: Align extraction fields with question and eligibility, pilot instructions and verify extraction; charting is not unstructured copying. [S09] |
| D08 | Deduplication | E: Remove duplicate records; link reports of one study while retaining supplementary details. [S07, S03] |
| D09 | Triangulation | E: Associated reports supply complementary information, not additional independent studies. [S03] |
| D10 | Contradiction handling | E: Investigate discrepant reports and corrections instead of selecting a convenient value. [S03] |
| D11 | Uncertainty | E: Effect certainty is outcome-specific and includes bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias, not just study count. [S13] |
| D12 | Analysis and synthesis | E: Scoping can use counts and basic content analysis; advanced effect or theory synthesis needs an appropriate different design. [S10] |
| D13 | Review and QA | E: Independent screening is preferred; JBI describes resource-constrained second-check alternatives. Neither an unreviewed single pass nor a duplicated AI prompt automatically meets that standard. [S05, S07; final sentence A] |
| D14 | Reporting | E: PRISMA specifies reporting, not review conduct or methodological quality assessment. Report actual methods and departures, not a checklist-shaped fiction. [S01; final sentence A] |
| D15 | Update behaviour | E: Justify an update, relate new searches and eligibility to the earlier review, and explain whether conclusions change or remain supported. [S11] |
| D16 | Failure modes | A: Undocumented narrowing, repeated study counting and scoping-to-advice overreach violate these distinctions. [S03, S08, S10] |
| D17 | Repair strategies | A: Repair search, study linkage or extraction locally; change method when scoping cannot support the requested inference. [S03, S09, S10, S11] |

## M3: Market and competitive intelligence

| ID | Dimension | Professional finding and bounded implication |

## Transfer assessment

The useful convergence is a set of responsibilities, not one common sequence: fit the question, preserve source context, distinguish units of evidence, examine contrary material, communicate uncertainty, review the inferential step and repair visibly. This is an analytical conclusion from M1-M9, constrained by the accepted Stage 1 boundary.

The important divergences must survive later design: comprehensive versus purposive discovery; mandatory versus question-dependent appraisal; factual verification versus causal or interpretative synthesis; publication versus private decision support; evidence preservation versus minimisation; and expert/team standards versus routine bounded research. Standards requiring qualified human review cannot be satisfied merely by relabelling multiple model outputs as independent experts.
