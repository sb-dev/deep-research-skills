# Stage 2 Draft Failure Taxonomy

These are candidate, evidence-informed failure classes, not measured frequencies or implemented validators. Each class supplies a diagnostic question and a bounded repair target. Sources resolve through the [source register](2026-09-10-stage-02-sources.md); the [method matrix](2026-09-10-stage-02-method-comparison.md) provides disciplinary context. Repairs are this bootstrap's design synthesis, not a claim that a source prescribes this exact taxonomy.

| ID | Failure and observable symptom | Diagnostic distinction | Smallest appropriate repair | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | Question displacement: a report answers a convenient neighbouring question. | Was the actual evidence need agreed, or only the topic? | Reframe the unanswered need and revisit only findings that depend on the wrong scope. | S16, M3 |
| F02 | Coverage inflation: selective searching is described as exhaustive. | Were sources and limits chosen before seeing the desired answer? | Complete the justified missing search or narrow the coverage claim and explain the limit. | S02, S06, S14 |
| F03 | Search invisibility: a reader cannot reconstruct why evidence was selected. | Missing documentation versus a genuinely deficient search. | Recover search/selection provenance; rerun only where the missing record prevents assurance. | S02, S07 |
| F04 | Access laundering: an abstract, snippet or catalogue entry is represented as the inspected original. | Which part was actually accessible? | Correct attribution and claim scope; obtain the relevant original where necessary. | S36; Stage 1 |
| F05 | Unit inflation: reports, reposts or related filings are counted as independent observations. | Duplicate file, repeated report, shared study, or meaningful new version? | Restore unit identity and recalculate only dependent counts or support claims. | S03, S07, S20 |
| F06 | False corroboration: several channels ultimately depend on one source. | Is there new observation, or only another transmission path? | Reclassify dependence; seek genuinely distinct support only where the claim requires it. | S24; Stage 1 |
| F07 | Context loss: a technically accurate quotation or value misrepresents its setting. | Did date, population, question wording, caption or surrounding text change its meaning? | Restore context and revise the affected interpretation. | S09, S37, S42 |
| F08 | Appraisal mismatch: mapping evidence is treated as proof of effectiveness, or prestige as method quality. | Does the claim require appraisal or certainty not supplied by this research design? | Add the necessary specialist assessment or reduce the inference, without inventing a quality score. | S08, S13 |
| F09 | Selective-result bias: adverse, null or inconvenient evidence disappears. | Was an eligibility rule applied consistently, or changed because of the result? | Restore eligible evidence and report how it affects the synthesis, including unchanged conclusions. | S07, S32 |
| F10 | Synthesis mismatch: incompatible quantities or meanings are combined. | Different units, populations, methods or substantive questions? | Regroup, separate findings or use a justified method; do not manufacture comparability. | S12, S14 |
| F11 | Significance counting: the majority of significant findings is reported as the effect. | Statistical significance versus effect direction, size and precision. | Return to the estimates and choose an appropriate synthesis rather than count favourable tests. | S12 |
| F12 | Uncertainty collapse: observations, inference, likelihood and confidence become one confident sentence. | Which component is known, estimated, assumed or unresolved? | Split the claim and explain the uncertainty that matters to its use. | S13, S28 |
| F13 | Identity/preservation confusion: a stored hash is offered as proof that an event happened. | File integrity, origin, contextual authenticity and event inference are separate checks. | Reopen the unsupported verification layer, preserving valid collection evidence. | S23 |
| F14 | Temporal substitution: retrieval date or a fresh publication disguises stale underlying evidence. | Publication, observation, effective period and access date differ. | Correct temporal labels and recheck only claims whose intended currency is unsupported. | S11, S22; Stage 1 |
| F15 | Blind-spot reinforcement: a fixed brief excludes potentially important emerging evidence. | Irrelevant noise versus a material challenge to the original need. | Flag the challenge to the requester; do not silently rewrite the agreed objective. | S40; Stage 1 |
| F16 | Assurance theatre: structural checks or repeated model answers are called independent substantive review. | Was the inference challenged with appropriate competence and a distinct review task? | Perform the missing review or state that the required assurance has not been obtained. | S05, S07, S30 |
| F17 | Authority or privacy overreach: research collection or publication exceeds permission. | Evidence usefulness does not itself grant access, contact or disclosure rights. | Stop the unauthorised operation, protect affected information and ask the appropriate owner. | S17, S25; Stage 1 |
| F18 | Silent revision: a material correction loses the original conclusion's history. | Correction of an error versus ordinary additional information. | Explain the affected claim and change, preserve valid work and notify relevant recipients. | S11, S27 |
| F19 | Deliverable invalidity: a correct analysis cannot support the intended decision. | Verification may pass while fitness for use fails. | Reassess applicability and communicate limits before the consumer commits. | S30 |
| F20 | Method-label inflation: a polished checklist report claims a methodology it did not perform. | Reporting compliance versus conduct, appraisal and actual execution. | Relabel honestly or execute the missing method; do not treat future work as completed. | S01, S15 |

## Interaction and repair order

These classes can co-occur. A false consensus might originate in F05, worsen through F06 and end as F12; rewriting prose alone would leave the data problem intact. Diagnose upstream evidence and transformation defects first, then revise downstream claims. This is a proposed repair principle grounded in the matrix and the accepted Stage 1 preservation requirement, not a selected orchestration state machine.

Use the smallest repair that restores the required support. Conversely, a wrongly framed question or incompatible synthesis method can require a broad reconsideration: preservation means retaining valid work, not refusing a necessary rerun. No class is cleared merely because a file passes structural validation.
